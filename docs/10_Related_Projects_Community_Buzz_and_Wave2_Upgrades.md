### 1.2 Technical Synthesis & Extracted Wave 2 Upgrades

1. **T-MAC / `bitnet.cpp` AVX2 `_mm256_shuffle_epi8` (`vpshufb`) 16-Entry LUT Micro-Kernel vs. 4x8 AVX2 FMA Unpacker:**
   * For a group of $g = 4$ binary weights $(b_0, b_1, b_2, b_3) \in \{0, 1\}^4$ packed into a 4-bit nibble $idx = \sum_{j=0}^3 b_j 2^j \in \{0, \dots, 15\}$, any 4 activations $(a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3)$ have only 16 possible signed partial sums:
     $$T[idx] = \sum_{j=0}^{3} (2 b_j - 1) \cdot a_j, \quad idx \in \{0, \dots, 15\}$$
   * By quantizing the 16 partial sums to `int16_t` and splitting them into a 16-byte low-byte table `lut_lo` and a 16-byte high-byte table `lut_hi` broadcast into both 128-bit lanes of two AVX2 `YMM` registers via `_mm256_broadcastsi128_si256`, two `_mm256_shuffle_epi8` (`vpshufb`) instructions evaluate **32 4-weight dot products (128 binary MACs)** in parallel!
   * **Wave 2 Integration (`kinesis_unified/sub2bit_gemm_and_smart_cache.hpp`):** We added `W1TmacLutPanelSet`, `pack_w1_tmac_lut_panels`, and `execute_tmac_lut_w1_gemm` alongside our exact FP32 `4x8` FMA unpacker (`execute_smart_w1_gemm`), optionally gated by the Tiled-CBC activation energy sieve. It hoists `lut_lo_256[32]` and `lut_hi_256[32]` (`_mm256_broadcastsi128_si256`) outside the panel loop and processes 4 panels (`32` columns) simultaneously via 256-bit 32-lane `_mm256_shuffle_epi8` (`F14`, with a 1-panel 128-bit `_mm_shuffle_epi8` tail path), verified bit-identical (`0.00e+00`) against its scalar int8-LUT reference and within the int8 rounding bound of FP32.

2. **Marlin L2 Cache Persistence (`cp.async.ca` vs `cp.async.cg`), Ping-Pong Staging & Small-Batch Stream-K (`BM = 16`):**
   * During small-batch decode ($M \le 16$), activations $A$ ($[M, K]$) are reused across all $N$ columns while weights $W$ ($[N, K]$) stream once. Loading $A$ with `cp.async.ca.shared.global` (cache in L1 + L2) and streaming $W$ with `cp.async.cg.shared.global` prevents streaming weights from evicting $A$ out of L2.
   * Furthermore, when $M \le 16$ and $N = 4096$, a 2D CTA grid with `BM=128, BN=256` launches only $16$ CTAs, leaving $112$ of $128$ SMs idle on RTX 4090 ($87.5\%$ idle SMs!).
   * **Wave 2 Integration (`fused_w1_hadacore/w1a16_hadacore_kernel.cu` & `sim_harness/smsp_roofline_sim.py`):** We implemented `cp_async_ca_16B`, double-buffered shared memory ping-pong staging (`smem_a_tile[2][128 * 128]`), `fused_w2a16_hadacore_gemm_kernel` (ternary 2-bit global kernel), and `marlin_streamk_w1a16_decode_kernel` (`BM=16` Split-K / Stream-K small-batch decode kernel with deterministic `marlin_streamk_reduce_slices_kernel` slice reduction `O16`), compiled and verified on the host via the 256-fiber CTA / 32-lane warp execution engine (`O1`), plus Stream-K wave-quantization modeling in `smsp_roofline_sim.py`.

3. **Kronecker-Hadamard ($H_p \otimes H_q$) Transform (`QuIP#`, `SpinQuant`, `HadaCore`):**
   * By the Kronecker mixed-product identity, for any activation vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^{p \cdot q}$ reshaped as $\text{Mat}_{p \times q}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times q}$:
     $$\text{Mat}_{p \times q}\big(x \cdot (H_p \otimes H_q)\big) = H_p^\top \cdot \text{Mat}_{p \times q}(x) \cdot H_q$$
   * For a $K = 32 = 2 \times 16$ warp step ($H_{32} = H_2 \otimes H_{16}$) or a $g = 128 = 8 \times 16$ quantization group ($H_{128} = H_8 \otimes H_{16}$), right-multiplication by $H_{16}$ executes on Tensor Cores via `hadacore_rotate_m16k16_in_regs`, and left-multiplication by $H_2$ or $H_8$ mixes the sub-tiles in registers via `fma.rn.bf16x2` butterflies!
   * **Wave 2 Integration (`w1a16_hadacore_kernel.cu`, `ptx_bf162_emu.c`, `spinquant_packer.py`):** We added `hadacore_kronecker_h32_m16k32_in_regs` in `w1a16_hadacore_kernel.cu`, full 2-stage $H_2 \otimes H_{16}$ + W2A16 ternary MMA verification in `ptx_bf162_emu.c` (`O12`, `F18`), and both `build_kernel_online_rotation` (exact fixed kernel rotation `F4`) and `optimize_kronecker_stiefel_rotation` (learned factorized Kronecker-Stiefel $R_{\text{kron}} = R_P \otimes H_{16}$ rotation `F13`, absorbed offline into $W_O' = (R_P^\top \otimes I_{16}) W_O$) in `spinquant_packer.py`.

### 2.2 Where `.Unified_Hardware_Accel` Leads & Wave 2 Upgrades

* **Existing Advantages in `.Unified_Hardware_Accel`:**
  1. ### 3.2 Extracted Wave 2 Upgrades Across Our Codebase

1. **Scale-Weighted Row-Maximum Holder Sub-Tile Sieve (`structural_filters/tiled_cbc_sieve.cu` & `tiled_cbc_sieve.py`):**
   * Combining Quest's upper-bound philosophy with PowerInfer's hot-tile weighting, we implemented `warp_reduce_rowmax_l1_k32`, `tiled_cbc_subtile_rowmax_bound_mask`, and `tiled_cbc_sieve_scale_weighted_sub32_mask`, which bound the worst-case output contribution of each $K=32$ sub-tile via the exact Holder $(L_1, L_\infty)$ inequality $\max_{m} \|A_{m, k32}\|_1 \cdot \alpha_{\max}$ (`O9`) and return a 4-bit mask across `BLOCK_K = 128` (executed and verified on the 32-lane warp-collective host engine).
2. **PowerInfer Offline Power-Law Calibration + Windowed Frequency Decay (`kinesis_unified/sub2bit_gemm_and_smart_cache.hpp`):**
   * Added `seed_offline_powerlaw_hot_panels` (pre-populating Tier-1 GPU VRAM at load time using offline calibration L1/frequency scores to eliminate cold-start compulsory misses) and `decay_access_frequencies` (windowed right-shift aging `count >>= 1` from PowerInfer-2 so topic shifts smoothly evict stale VRAM panels).
3. **LServe Hybrid Static Streaming Heads + Dynamic Quest Retrieval Heads (`structural_filters/lserve_sparse_attn.py`):**

4. **Q-GaLore INT4 Stochastic-Rounded Projectors, Lazy Cosine-Similarity Schedule & 2-Step QR Subspace Iteration (`qat_galore_engine/galore_async_offload.py`):**
